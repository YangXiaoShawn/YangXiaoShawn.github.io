# Causal Marketplace Lab: technical report

**Evidence status:** the empirical description—including the manifested weather, ecological
neighborhood-income, and permitted-event layers—predeclared semi-synthetic sensitivity benchmark,
HTE recovery audit, honest-holdout policy comparison, and automated reports were generated by the
verified reproduction. The current machine-generated report is
`artifacts/reports/technical_report_generated.md`; this file is its methodological companion.  
**Decision context:** rider discounts or driver incentives in a two-sided ride-hailing marketplace.  
**Primary decision target:** market-level incremental completed trips or welfare per incremental dollar, including spillovers and persistence over a declared horizon.

## Executive summary

A marketplace experiment is not a conventional independent-unit A/B test. Treating a rider can change driver location, wait time, congestion, nearby demand, and later periods. Consequently, an individually randomized comparison can be internally correct for a direct offer effect yet answer the wrong rollout question.

This project separates five evidence layers:

1. Public trip records plus separately pinned NOAA weather, ACS neighborhood-income, and NYC
   permitted-event/calendar sources describe observed completed-trip, flow, fare, weather,
   ecological area-income, and permit-intensity associations. They do **not** identify causal
   price, weather, income, event, or incentive responses.
2. A semi-synthetic marketplace makes effects known by construction and permits honest
   comparisons of experiment designs and estimators; its NYC-informed variant borrows only
   descriptive initialization moments.
3. A second known-truth benchmark borrows a fixed, pre-treatment NYC OD graph as exposure
   geometry. Its graph weights are not spillover strength, and its controlled exposure slopes are
   not automatically the market-total policy effect.
4. A theoretical fixed-point layer solves paired counterfactual equilibria inside declared
   equations whose behavioral parameters are not estimated from NYC.
5. A budget-constrained policy layer converts validated simulator effects into an allocation
   decision and evaluates that decision on simulation draws not used to fit it.

The conditional design recommendation is to randomize geographic markets, or geographic markets over randomized time blocks, when cross-unit interference is material. Treatment duration and washout should be selected from persistence sensitivity and power calculations rather than chosen by convention. The rollout decision target is the market-level policy contrast; a randomized-arm intent-to-treat contrast is reported separately and only interpreted as that rollout target when the exposure, carryover, and budget assumptions make the two coincide. Uncertainty must align to the assignment unit. Individual randomization remains useful for a direct-response mechanism question when market exposure is fixed or negligible.

No generated quantity in this companion should be treated as a live-market effect. The verified
artifacts report simulator recovery and holdout results; the full-grid gate withholds a robust
design recommendation, and the HTE learner fails its oracle constant-effect recovery gate.

## 1. Research question and evidence hierarchy

The product decision has three linked parts:

- **Design:** What should be randomized so that the experiment captures rather than hides marketplace spillovers?
- **Inference:** Which estimator recovers the declared effect with calibrated uncertainty under spatial and temporal dependence?
- **Allocation:** Given a fixed budget, where should treatment be delivered after the experiment?

The hierarchy below prevents a common category error.

| Layer | Data | Permitted claim | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Empirical description | Public Chicago TNP records; bounded/full-month NYC TLC records; pinned NOAA, ACS/geography, and permit/holiday sources | Fare, trip composition, published-trip temporal patterns, geography, flows, missingness, weather, ecological area-income, and permit-intensity associations | Calling any contextual contrast causal; treating area income as individual income, permits as attendance, OD flows as substitution effects, or completed trips as latent demand |
| Semi-synthetic validation | Simulated riders, drivers, zones, time blocks, assignments, and potential market paths, optionally initialized by descriptive NYC moments | Estimator recovery and design performance relative to known simulator ground truth | Treating an NYC-informed simulated magnitude as an NYC effect or structurally estimated response |
| NYC-geometry known truth | Fixed pre-treatment NYC OD graph plus randomized two-stage saturation and a declared synthetic outcome model | Recovery of controlled own, mapped-neighbor, and exact-history effects | Treating graph weight as spillover strength or scoring a target-mismatched coefficient as the market-total effect |
| Theoretical fixed point | Declared two-sided equilibrium equations and paired control/policy solutions | Within-model equilibrium counterfactuals, residuals, convergence, and contraction diagnostics | Calling the solution an empirical equilibrium, NYC structural estimate, or live forecast |
| Decision projection | Validated effect scenarios, cost ledger, policy rules, and holdout simulations | Conditional value and budget efficiency under stated assumptions | Guaranteeing a live return or choosing a policy on its training sample |

The estimand definitions and their identification conditions are maintained in `docs/estimands.md`. Every generated result should identify the evidence layer, target estimand, scenario, seed range, design, estimator, and weighting rule.

## 3. Empirical marketplace panel

### 3.1 Role of the public data

The empirical panel has two defensible roles:

- document marketplace scale, seasonality, geographic concentration, flow structure, missingness, and rounding; and
- provide broad calibration targets for the simulator, such as zone-time trip intensity and trip-characteristic distributions.

It does not reveal the causal effect of an unobserved counterfactual discount or incentive policy. Average fare rises with distance and duration and can also respond to demand, congestion, product mix, geography, and unobserved supply tightness. Demand and price are simultaneously determined. Even a richly adjusted regression can retain selection, simultaneity, and measurement bias.

### 3.2 Implemented panel contract

The sample configuration aggregates pickup Chicago community area by one-hour local reported-time bins, with configurable finer intervals for other extracts. Missing or outside-city endpoints use the explicit `__MISSING_OR_OUTSIDE__` category. The implemented zone-time panel includes:

- trip count, distinct drop-off zones, outbound trip count, and intra-zone share;
- average, median, and total fare;
- average trip duration and average/total trip miles;
- shared-request and shared-match shares, and airport-trip share where observed;
- pickup-geography missing-or-suppressed share and declared timestamp/fare rounding shares;
- service date, year, month, day of week, hour, minute, and weekend indicator; and
- `panel_grain` plus `evidence_label = descriptive_real_data`.

A separate origin-destination panel stores origin zone, destination zone, time bin, trip count, average fare, and average trip miles. The raw cache, clean trips, zone-time panel, and OD panel are written as partitioned artifacts with a portable configuration and file hashes in `data/manifest.json`; `data/diagnostics.json` records missingness, suppression/nonreporting, rounding, and validity checks.

Optional enrichments must fail closed: the core pipeline should run without them, and reports should not silently convert missing enrichments into measured zeros.

The full-month neighborhood bundle uses the 2022 ACS five-year B19001 distribution rather than a
median of geographic medians. Official tract rows map to 2020 NTAs, and all sixteen household
income bins are allocated to overlapping Taxi Zones in EPSG:6933 with within-NTA normalization.
The manifest verifies household/bin and published-trip conservation and reports classified-trip
coverage after keeping unsupported and non-residential-dominant zones unclassified. The resulting
high/low labels describe residential-area composition: they are not rider or driver income, and
land use, population, employment, transit access, time patterns, and selection into completed
trips confound the comparison.

The event bundle retains the complete official query result for permits overlapping January:
6,007 source rows and 951 source event IDs. Of those, 5,998 positive-duration rows representing
949 IDs expand under half-open `[start, end)` semantics to 6,711 unique event-date records. One
reversed and eight zero-duration intervals remain visible but are excluded rather than silently
repaired or assigned invented duration.
The daily signal is citywide and repeats across a date's 24 panel hours—it is not zone-level or
event-hour exposure, and permit records do not measure attendance. Days above the source-only
monthly median permit count show +3.055% more published completed trips than lower-intensity days
in the raw contrast. All eight weekends are in the high group; among weekdays the contrast is
-8.198%. This composition reversal is a diagnostic against causal interpretation, not evidence
that events reduce weekday demand.

Privacy suppression, rounded timestamps, and rounded monetary values are measurement processes, not cosmetic issues. Diagnostics should report their prevalence and test whether conclusions change at coarser spatial or temporal aggregation.

For the Chicago source, timestamps are declared rounded to the nearest 15 minutes, fares to the nearest $2.50, and tips to the nearest $1.00. Missing census tracts may reflect privacy suppression or out-of-city travel. The cleaned schema retains declared rounding increments, grid checks, and missing-or-suppressed indicators rather than manufacturing precision.

### 3.4 Generated calibration and uncalibrated causal parameters

`artifacts/reports/calibration.json` uses the descriptive mean completed trips per observed cell and trip-count-weighted mean observed fare as illustrative scale anchors. It rescales latent demand and supply together until the simulator's control-path completed-trip mean matches that sparse-cell statistic, preserving the template's assumed supply-to-demand relationship. The fixture selects a fixed number of lexical-ID rows in each time stratum and omits unobserved cells, so this match is an internal traceability check—not a representative city calibration. Duration, distance, and pooling are recorded as trip-count-weighted comparison targets but do not currently change parameters. Capacity, matching, rider value, treatment response, spillover, persistence, substitution, driver mobility, and noise remain configured structural assumptions. The artifact is labeled `illustrative_empirical_scale_anchor`; it does not estimate a discount response or validate the behavioral model.

The separate NYC full-month bundle provides a stronger descriptive scale anchor. It verifies the
complete 262-zone × 744-hour panel, request-to-pickup time, trip/fare/pay moments, variance
components, exact-lag associations, and an OD-flow network. The portable NYC simulation proposal
matches a deterministic control path to mean completed trips, mean request-to-pickup time, and
the descriptive between-zone and hour-of-day variance shares. It still leaves treatment response,
available supply, matching, spillovers, persistence, substitution, and welfare as explicit
assumptions. The OD map is a pre-treatment exposure-map candidate, not an estimated spillover
function; temporal correlation is not assigned to simulator persistence.

Because the fixture is a fixed 25-row extract at each selected timestamp rather than a query-complete census, the default sample panel contains occupied cells only. A missing zone-hour in this fixture is unknown—not evidence of zero demand. The code supports and tests a gap-free complete grid, but zero filling is reserved for extracts whose query coverage makes an absent cell interpretable as zero.

## 4. Semi-synthetic marketplace

### 4.1 Purpose

Simulation solves an identification-audit problem that observational data cannot: for each randomized experiment, the system can compute the same target under counterfactual market paths. Ground truth is defined using paired potential paths and common random numbers, which makes the contrast explicit and reduces irrelevant simulation noise.

The simulator is **semi-synthetic** because broad baseline moments can be calibrated to the public panel while treatment-response, substitution, supply-movement, congestion, spillover, and persistence parameters remain declared modeling assumptions. Calibration is not structural identification.

### 4.2 Required state and behavior

The marketplace state contains geographic zones and time periods, rider demand, driver supply, matching or service probability, and a congestion or waiting mechanism. `SimulationConfig.treatment_version` selects `rider_discount`, `driver_incentive`, or `bundled`. Rider-only paths disable the incentive and supply-response channels; driver-only paths disable the discount and demand-response channels; the bundle activates both at a common assigned intensity. Driver-side log-response channels scale linearly with `incentive_per_driver / reference_incentive_per_driver`, while cost uses the configured payment. This explicit dose mapping is a sensitivity assumption, not an empirical supply elasticity. Each run has one version—simultaneous factorial rider-versus-driver arms would require a separate multi-arm assignment design. The transition logic permits:

- riders to substitute across time or zones;
- drivers to reposition toward expected earnings or incentives;
- own-zone and neighboring treatment exposure;
- short-run and decaying persistent effects;
- market-level capacity constraints; and
- a hard policy budget.

This is a disciplined scenario engine, not a claim that one behavioral specification uniquely represents a live marketplace. Each substantive parameter should have a baseline, a sensitivity range, and a clear unit.

### 4.3 Ground truth

For a specified regime and horizon, ground truth is the difference between outcomes under two policy paths applied to matched latent shocks. Relevant truths include direct, spillover, market-total, short-run, cumulative, and efficiency effects. A benchmark is valid only when its estimator target matches the truth being used for bias calculations.

Determinism is tested at the interface: the same configuration and seed should reproduce the same simulated market and benchmark inputs. Different seeds should produce new stochastic draws without changing the scenario definition.

## 5. Experiment designs

The benchmark design set should be compared under common target estimands and budget rules.

| Design | What is randomized | Best use | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual | Rider or driver eligibility | Direct offer response at a fixed saturation | Shared-market exposure contaminates controls and misses system-wide effects |
| Geographic cluster | Whole zones or connected zone groups | Market-total effect when clusters contain spillovers | Cross-boundary rider and driver movement |
| Time block | Marketplace treatment by time interval | Reversible short-run response | Time shocks, anticipation, and carryover |
| Switchback | Randomized treatment sequence within market | Efficient within-market comparison when washout is credible | Persistent effects contaminate later control blocks |
| Geo × time cluster | Geographic clusters over randomized blocks | Spatial and temporal variation with exposure modeling | Few effective clusters and complex assignment probabilities |

Design comparison should vary number and size of clusters, duration, saturation, washout, budget, spillover strength, and persistence. A design that appears precise only because it ignores the assignment unit is not efficient; it is miscalibrated.

The operational recommendation is conditional:

- prefer parallel geographic clusters when enough weakly connected markets can contain the dominant spillovers;
- prefer a geo × time or switchback design when geography is scarce, the intervention is reversible, and a sensitivity-supported washout is feasible; and
- use individual assignment only when the direct effect is the intended estimand or interference is demonstrably negligible/controlled.

## 6. Estimator ladder

Complex estimators are benchmarked against transparent baselines.

1. **Difference in means.** A design-aligned ITT benchmark. It is valid only with the correct analysis level and weighting.
2. **Regression adjustment.** Adds pre-treatment prognostic variables or design strata to improve precision; specification must not condition on post-treatment marketplace states.
3. **Cluster-aware estimation.** Uses assignment-level uncertainty and accommodates within-cluster correlation; few clusters require randomization inference or a small-sample method rather than asymptotic confidence alone.
4. **Difference in differences.** Appropriate only with a credible untreated counterfactual trend, no anticipation, stable composition, and interference contained or modeled.
5. **Doubly robust estimation.** Combines outcome and assignment/propensity models under overlap; randomization probabilities remain preferable to estimated propensities when known.
6. **Synthetic-control-style comparison.** Useful for a small number of aggregated treated markets with a credible, uncontaminated donor pool and good pre-treatment fit.
7. **Heterogeneous-effect estimation.** Exploratory unless pre-specified; uses pre-treatment modifiers and honest evaluation.
8. **Optional causal forest or meta-learner.** An extension, not a substitute for design-aligned benchmarks.

No method repairs a mismatch between the estimator and estimand. In particular, flexible prediction does not correct exposure misclassification or cross-cluster interference by itself.

## 7. Monte Carlo benchmark

### 7.1 Protocol

For each scenario, the benchmark should:

1. freeze the data-generating parameters, estimand, exposure mapping, and design;
2. generate known ground truth from paired counterfactual paths;
3. repeat assignment and outcome generation across deterministic, non-overlapping seeds;
4. apply each estimator without access to the ground truth;
5. store one row per scenario-design-estimator-replication or a traceable aggregation; and
6. aggregate performance with Monte Carlo uncertainty.

Primary benchmark metrics are

\[
\operatorname{Bias}(\hat\tau)=\mathbb{E}[\hat\tau]-\tau,
\]

\[
\operatorname{RMSE}(\hat\tau)=
\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[(\hat\tau-\tau)^2]},
\]

coverage $=\Pr(\tau\in CI)$, and power $=\Pr(\text{reject }H_0)$ under a declared alternative. Variance and cost per unit of decision-relevant information should also be reported. Monte Carlo standard errors and replication counts must accompany comparisons.

### 7.2 Required validation scenarios

- **No interference / no persistence:** a valid design-aligned estimator should recover its target within Monte Carlo uncertainty.
- **Interference:** a naive individual contrast can target a direct or diluted exposure contrast rather than the market-total effect; the benchmark should show the mismatch rather than relabel it.
- **Persistence:** a short-washout switchback can be biased for the contemporaneous effect; longer washouts trade information quantity for cleaner contrasts.
- **Budget pressure:** assignment and policy comparisons must enforce the same budget accounting.

### 7.3 Generated benchmark status

The verified benchmark spans the predeclared spillover, persistence, duration, cluster-count,
saturation, washout, shared-budget, and treatment-version cells. Its fit ledger preserves every
planned, successful, failed, and predeclared-inapplicable fit. No design-estimator pair is
applicable, complete, identified, and inference-valid across the full grid, so the automated
report correctly withholds a robust rollout recommendation. A separate known-truth two-stage
saturation benchmark recovers controlled own, mapped-neighbor, and exact-history responses; its
naive assignment coefficient is deliberately not scored against the market-total policy truth.
Scenario-level counts, values, and Monte Carlo uncertainty are generated in
`artifacts/reports/technical_report_generated.md` and
`artifacts/benchmarks/benchmark_results.csv`; they are not copied into this source narrative.

### 7.4 NYC-shaped, equilibrium, and contextual descriptive evidence

These extensions answer different questions and must not be pooled into one claim:

| Layer | Input used | Valid interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| NYC-informed benchmark | Hash-verified descriptive scale and heterogeneity anchor | Known-truth design/estimator recovery under explicit simulator assumptions; not an NYC causal effect |
| NYC graph benchmark | Reproducibly selected subgraph of the fixed pre-treatment NYC OD geometry | Known-truth controlled own/neighbor/history recovery; OD weights determine relative exposure mapping, not spillover strength |
| Equilibrium benchmark | Declared two-sided fixed-point equations and parameters | Theoretical paired control/policy truth conditional on the model; not an empirical or NYC structural estimate |
| NOAA weather enrichment | Pinned Central Park station observations joined to the January panel | Descriptive completed-trip/weather association only; not a causal weather effect or instrument |
| ACS neighborhood-income enrichment | Official 2022 five-year B19001 distributions plus equal-area Taxi Zone/NTA mapping | Ecological high/low-area description with explicit exclusions; not individual income or an income effect |
| NYC permitted-event enrichment | Complete January permit-overlap snapshot plus official holiday dates | Citywide daily permit/calendar association; permits are not attendance, and raw/weekday contrasts are not event effects |

Each benchmark artifact carries its own evidence label, manifest, input hashes, configuration,
records, summary, fit ledger, failures, and metadata. The NYC-shaped artifact readers revalidate
their anchor or calibration source rather than trusting a detached result table.

## 8. Heterogeneity and budget-constrained policy

Candidate effect modifiers include baseline demand, market tightness, time of day, weather, neighborhood characteristics, and event intensity. All must be measured before the treatment whose effect they modify. Post-treatment wait time, realized price, driver location, or redemption should not be used as if they were baseline targeting features.

The policy comparison should include:

- no treatment;
- random allocation under the same expected budget;
- uniform allocation;
- a transparent rule based on pre-treatment state; and
- model-based targeting with a complexity or instability penalty.

Model selection occurs on training simulations. Final value, incremental outcome, spend, and budget feasibility are measured on unseen seeds or a held-out simulation set. The system should compare policies by rerunning the complete reduced-form market simulator rather than summing isolated conditional effects. A policy is not deployable if its apparent gain disappears under modest spillover, persistence, cost, or calibration perturbations. This is a modeled system response, not a solved strategic equilibrium.

**Generated policy result:** under the configured $1,000 cap and eight disjoint holdout-market
seeds, the conservative rule selects uniform allocation (mean 68.4967 incremental trips,
SE 0.1050, p10 68.1030 per modeled market). Model targeting underperforms random allocation.
This is a simulator-conditional comparison, not a live lift or return forecast.

The HTE learner is likewise a negative result: RMSE 0.0417 exceeds the oracle constant-effect
baseline RMSE of 0.0247, so it is not decision-ready.

## 9. Inference and identification audit

The most consequential threats are:

- **price endogeneity:** demand, price, supply, and product mix are jointly determined in public records;
- **interference:** controls can be exposed through shared riders, drivers, geography, or flows;
- **carryover:** treatment can affect later blocks through repositioning, learning, or delayed demand;
- **market-boundary leakage:** measured gains may be substitution from areas outside the outcome ledger;
- **few clusters:** nominal sample size can greatly exceed effective randomized sample size;
- **exposure misspecification:** adjacency may be a poor proxy for flow-mediated spillovers;
- **suppression and rounding:** apparent zeroes or exact timestamps may be source artifacts;
- **ecological allocation:** NTA household-income distributions do not reveal rider/driver income,
  and equal-area allocation cannot remove residential sorting or land-use confounding;
- **calendar-proxy confounding:** permitted-event counts do not measure attendance or precise
  exposure, and their January high-intensity group is perfectly entangled with weekend status;
- **transportability:** a calibrated Chicago semi-synthetic scenario need not represent another city, season, or platform;
- **welfare incompleteness:** trip records omit rider surplus, driver opportunity cost, and some externalities; and
- **policy overfitting:** flexible targeting can exploit simulation noise or a narrow scenario grid.

The companion `reports/identification_and_limitations.md` provides design-specific assumptions, diagnostics, falsification tests, and stop conditions.

## 10. Decision rule

Before launch, pre-specify:

- the market-level rollout policy contrast and, separately where available, the randomized-arm ITT from the registry;
- the primary completed-trip, profit, or welfare outcome and cost denominator;
- service, driver, geographic-equity, and spend guardrails;
- assignment unit, exposure map, treatment saturation, and weights;
- experiment horizon and washout justified by sensitivity and power;
- the cluster-aware estimator and uncertainty method; and
- the minimum decision threshold, including practical rather than only statistical significance.

A go decision requires a positive decision-relevant effect with uncertainty excluding an unacceptable downside, no guardrail breach, stable conclusions across credible exposure/persistence assumptions, and an honest-holdout policy that improves on simple budget-matched baselines. Statistical significance alone is insufficient.

## 11. What is and is not supported

### Supported by the design of the project

- Marketplace interference can change both the appropriate estimand and randomization unit.
- Public trip records can provide descriptive calibration without identifying incentive effects.
- Known-ground-truth simulation can test whether an estimator answers its claimed causal question.
- Fixed NYC OD geometry can stress-test an exposure-mapped estimator without estimating spillover
  strength from flows.
- A fixed-point solver can test feedback logic inside a declared theoretical model without claiming
  an empirically estimated equilibrium.
- Geographic or geo × time designs are defensible candidates when spillovers are material.
- Policy evaluation requires a common budget cap, jointly simulated market-system outcomes, and an honest holdout.

### Generated but conditional evidence

- empirical fixture moments and missingness/rounding diagnostics;
- full-month NYC weather, ecological neighborhood-income, and permitted-event associations,
  labeled descriptive and noncausal;
- NYC-informed and NYC-geometry estimator recovery against simulator-known truth;
- theoretical fixed-point counterfactuals inside the declared, unfitted equations;
- simulator ground truths and design/estimator recovery by declared scenario;
- conditional dashboard recommendation for an exactly matched no-interference cell; and
- honest-holdout modeled policy value, spend, and efficiency under the configured cap.

The full grid does not support one robust design. Live duration, washout, power, causal lift,
welfare, and return remain unresolved.

### Unsupported without additional evidence

- a causal fare elasticity from the observational panel;
- a causal weather effect or valid weather instrument from the Central Park join;
- individual rider/driver income or a causal income effect from the ACS area allocation;
- attendance, realized event exposure, or a causal event effect from permit records;
- a spillover-strength estimate from NYC OD weights;
- an empirical NYC equilibrium or structurally estimated behavioral response;
- a live-market treatment magnitude inferred from simulator calibration;
- a universal claim that one randomization design dominates;
- a welfare conclusion when important surplus or cost components are absent; and
- transport from the Chicago sample to NYC or another platform without revalidation.
