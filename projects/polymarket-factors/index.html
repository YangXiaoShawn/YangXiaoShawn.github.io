Research question

Which prediction-market signals survive a realistic backtest?

Bounded prices, early settlement, and stale quotes can create apparent predictability. I study which cross-sectional signals remain after correcting those artifacts.

01 · Method

I study 4,000 Polymarket markets, remove five backtest artifacts, control for price levels, and evaluate factor signs on a held-out half of the panel.

02 · Conclusion

Reversal, volatility, and illiquidity survive the corrections.

Five backtest artifacts inflate 30-day momentum IC eightfold (−0.440 to −0.054); price-neutral OOS ICs remain significant for illiquidity (−0.072), volatility (−0.069), and reversal (−0.032).

Scope of the evidence. Backtest association, not live execution. Capacity is roughly $100–200 per position. The combined strategy weight was chosen in sample, and daily-close fills omit intraday slippage.

Research question and prediction-market mechanics

Question: Do behavioral biases leave predictable cross-sectional structure in prediction-market prices once clipping, return-base, skip-day, early-settlement, and stale-quote artifacts are removed?

Prediction-market prices represent bounded probabilities p ∈ (0, 1). Percentage returns explode near zero, shorting YES requires buying NO at cost (1 − p), and return distributions near 0 or 1 are mechanically skewed. Every factor is therefore evaluated both on raw probability-point changes (Δp) and price-neutral residuals (forward Δp orthogonalized on [1, p, p²]).

Capabilities demonstrated

  1. Eliminate five sequential backtest artifacts: price clipping, percentage-return base distortion, shared formation-day endpoint noise, early-settlement survival bias, and forward-filled stale entry quotes.
  2. Residualize forward probability changes on [1, p, p²] and run Fama-MacBeth regressions with Newey-West standard errors (6 lags) to separate genuine cross-sectional alpha from boundary skewness.
  3. Reconstruct market-specific effective spreads directly from on-chain YES/NO taker fills (median 0.4¢, p75 1.0¢).
  4. Test 24-hour sibling-market lead-lag across 690 multi-leg event markets and document the negative result (IC = 0.004, t = 0.3).

Evidence boundary

  • Backtest association, not live execution; candidate markets trade $1.6k–$12k daily, limiting capacity to roughly $100–$200 per position.
  • Daily-close fill assumptions omit intraday order-book impact.
  • The 35-combination three-factor ranking and the 50/50 combined strategy weighting were chosen on the evaluation sample and are not out-of-sample results.
