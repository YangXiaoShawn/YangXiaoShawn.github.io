Research question

Can machine learning time equity factors?

Factor returns vary over time. I test whether learned forecasts add value beyond a pooled, shrinkage-mean benchmark in emerging markets.

01 · Method

I compare out-of-sample linear, tree, and neural-network forecasts on JKP global factors, test developed-to-emerging-market transfer, and evaluate timing after turnover costs.

02 · Conclusion

Pooling explains much of the apparent predictability.

On capped value-weighted factors, all learned forecasts lose to the shrinkage mean on out-of-sample R². At the baseline trading cost, the shrinkage-mean tilt also beats learned timing portfolios.

Scope of the evidence. Out-of-sample predictive association. Results depend on factor weighting and trading costs; equal-weighted factors differ. Factor returns exclude their own rebalancing costs.

What I built

I built an out-of-sample evaluation and transfer-learning framework for 153 Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen (JKP) equity factors across 22 emerging and 23 developed markets, pairing Monte Carlo size/power audits with turnover-penalized portfolio tests.

Research question and benchmark discipline

Question: Is the predictability of equity factor returns documented in developed markets present in emerging markets, and does a model trained on developed-market history forecast emerging-market factor returns out of sample, net of trading costs?

In a multi-country factor panel, each country-factor mean is estimated from a noisy history. Testing against each series own expanding mean (hist_mean) conflates time-series factor timing with cross-sectional pooling of unconditional premia. On known-truth synthetic panels with zero predictability, testing against the own mean rejects 87.5% of the time; replacing it with a regional shrinkage mean (shrink_mean) restores nominal 5.0% size while keeping 92.5–100% power.

Capabilities demonstrated

  1. Audit benchmark size and power on synthetic multi-country panels before opening real emerging-market holdouts.
  2. Train pooled OLS, Elastic Net, gradient-boosted trees (GBRT), and 3-seed neural-network ensembles on deviations from the regional shrinkage mean.
  3. Compare three training scopes for the 22 emerging markets: EM-only (own), developed-to-emerging transfer (dm_only), and pooled.
  4. Evaluate both statistical accuracy (out-of-sample R² and Clark-West t-statistics) and net economic value after 20 bp turnover costs.
  5. Run systematic robustness across shrinkage intensity (κ = 60, 120, 240), equal-weighted vs uncapped value-weighted factors, time-varying MSCI reclassifications, and U.S. macro state variables.

Evidence boundary

  • Out-of-sample predictive comparison (association), not causal identification of risk premia or behavioral mechanisms.
  • JKP factor returns are gross of their own stock-level rebalancing costs and signed by the original studies; Sharpe ratio levels must be read comparatively.
  • Baseline market classification is static MSCI; historical reclassification robustness does not model initial index inclusion dates.
