# Ten-minute presentation outline

Audience: economists or a technical hiring panel. One slide per section.
Timings are cumulative.

---

## 0:00 — The question (1 min)

When the U.S. imposes product-level tariffs:

- Who bears the cost — the foreign exporter, or the U.S. importer?
- How fast does sourcing move to other countries?
- Which domestic industries gain, and which pay more for inputs?

Setting: Section 301 against China, 2018–2019. Three waves, staggered effective
dates, product-level variation.

**Say up front:** the Census API needs a key I don't have. No number here is an
empirical finding about U.S. trade. Here's what *is* real, and what I learned
building the instrument.

## 1:00 — Why tariff data is harder than it looks (2 min)

Four distinctions that decide whether an answer means anything:

| | |
|---|---|
| Customs value vs CIF | customs value excludes freight |
| Customs unit value vs landed unit value | tariff-exclusive vs duty-inclusive — **incidence needs both** |
| Announcement vs effective date | List 3's increase: announced for 1 Jan 2019, effective 10 May 2019 |
| Full vs partial tariff lines | 11 List 3 lines are covered *except* for named 10-digit numbers |

And: a customs unit value is **not a price**. It's value over quantity across a
heterogeneous bundle.

## 3:00 — The schedule reconciles exactly (2 min)

The annexes are in a 219-page typeset PDF; the XML has them as graphics.

**Point:** the 0.2% gaps were three different substantive errors, not noise.

## 5:00 — The methodological finding (3 min)

Validated the estimators against data with a known DGP.

| Parameter | Truth | Recovered |
|---|---|---|
| Exporter pass-through | −0.050 | **−0.0485** ✅ |
| Quantity response | −1.339 | −1.686 ❌ |

Why the quantity miss? My control group included third-country suppliers of
treated products. **They aren't untreated** — the tariff pushes demand toward
them. No-interference is violated, and the comparison group rises for the same
reason the treated group falls.

Re-estimate with treated-country flows of never-treated products only:
**−1.256**, close to truth. Price parameter unaffected under both — exactly as
predicted, since the generator has no third-country price spillover.

The pipeline now reports both control groups on every run.

## 8:00 — Two channels, never netted (1 min)

Pre-treatment BEA input-output weights → four exposure channels.

Of 72 industries, **8 face output protection and higher imported-input costs at
once** — primary metals, fabricated metal, machinery, motor vehicles.

A single "net exposure" number hides exactly the distributional question the
analysis exists to answer. The column that computes it is literally named
`net_contrast_do_not_use_alone`.

## Anticipated questions

**"Why not just use the synthetic results?"** They measure my code, not the
world. Presenting them as findings would be fabrication regardless of labelling.

**"Isn't the control-group problem obvious?"** In hindsight. It only surfaced
because I built a case where I knew the answer — which is the argument for doing
that routinely.

**"Why HS6 rather than HS10?"** Section 301 is legislated at HS8. HS6 loses
that, so 598 partly covered headings are excluded from both groups rather than
assigned an invented rate. HS10 with a key is the better panel.

**"Where's the structural model?"** Deliberately not built. Calibrating one on
synthetic flows would produce counterfactuals with the appearance of substance
and none of the content.
