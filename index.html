01 Experiments & incentives

Who should get the offer?

A discount can increase activity. The harder question is whether a model can identify who will respond—and spend the same budget better.

What I found

The complex targeting model has not earned its advantage.

How I tested it. I test effect estimates against known answers, then compare spending rules at the same budget in simulated markets held out from training.

Semi-synthetic validation, not a measured effect of a real NYC promotion. The oracle knows the true average effect; it is a test benchmark, not a deployable policy.

02 Forecasting & information

Would the forecast work in real time?

Economic statistics are revised after publication. A model can look better in hindsight if its backtest uses numbers no one knew at the time.

What I found

Changing the data vintage changes which models look best.

How I tested it. I reconstruct official release histories and rerun forecasts with three information rules: data known then, revised values with the same availability, and unrestricted latest values.

The fixed-mask comparison is a hindsight diagnostic, not a valid real-time forecast. Results describe this pilot and its final holdout; they do not establish a universally best model.

03 Housing & household finance

Does a cheap mortgage keep a loan in place?

When a new mortgage costs more than the one a homeowner already has, giving up the old loan becomes expensive. I study whether those loans are less likely to be paid off.

What I found

Bigger rate gaps are associated with fewer mortgage exits.

How I tested it. I compare monthly payoff rates across rate gaps, then estimate a complementary log-log hazard model with observed loan and local-market controls.

An association, not a causal estimate. Payoff and maturity are pooled; refinancing-related and sale-related payoff cannot be distinguished. Household moves are not observed. This is a selected Freddie Mac sample.

04 Trade & public policy

Who absorbed the tariff?

When the U.S. raises import tariffs, foreign suppliers could cut their prices—or importers could pay more. The distinction determines who bears the initial cost.

What I found

The evidence points toward higher costs for U.S. importers.

How I tested it. I compare each wave of tariffed products with never-tariffed products, tracking unit import values before and after the policy and checking three sample windows.

Unit values are not individual transaction prices. The duty-inclusive measure includes the tariff by construction; the pre-duty series is essential. Quantity fails its date-placebo check.

05 Market microstructure

Can a trading signal survive its costs?

Predicting a tiny price move is not the same as earning a return. Fees and execution delays can consume the entire apparent advantage.

What I found

After fees, none of the 144 scenarios remains positive.

How I tested it. I replay one four-hour BTC/ETH order-book capture across two evaluation phases, four horizons, and nine delay combinations, with a fixed 4 bp fee.

Exploratory simulation · research reference only · not live trading. The 144 scenarios overlap and are not independent experiments or a portfolio to add together.
